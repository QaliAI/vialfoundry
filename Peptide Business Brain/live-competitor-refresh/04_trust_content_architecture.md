# 04 — Trust Content Architecture

Prepared: 2026-06-07  
Purpose: recommend high-trust pages that make the site credible, conversion-focused, SEO/AEO/GEO-friendly, and compliance-aware without copying competitor text.

## Evidence base

- A2Z Peptides and BioLongevity Labs both use “how to read a COA” style content to educate buyers and build trust. Sources: https://a2zpeptides.com/1000394/how-to-read-peptide-coa-purity-identity-quality/ ; https://biolongevitylabs.com/quality/how-to-read-peptide-coa-quality/
- PeptideResearches has a research/quality page explaining HPLC, MS, COAs, and storage. Source: https://www.peptideresearches.com/en/research
- Ion Peptide and SwissChems maintain public test-result/lab-result pages. Sources: https://ionpeptide.com/lab-results/ ; https://swisschems.is/independent-test-results/
- Payment processors and guides emphasize clean product language, policy pages, documentation, and no human-use claims. Sources: https://allaypay.com/industries/peptides-merchant-services/ ; https://verifiedcreditcardprocessing.com/research-peptides-guide/ ; https://www.vectorpayments.com/peptide-payment-processing/

## Recommended page architecture

### 1. How to Read a COA

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Teach research buyers how to evaluate certificates of analysis without making product claims. |
| Audience | Research buyers, procurement reviewers, lab managers, cautious first-time buyers. |
| Search intent | “how to read peptide COA,” “peptide certificate of analysis,” “COA purity identity.” |
| Why it builds trust | Shows the brand understands documentation and helps buyers verify quality instead of asking for blind trust. |
| What not to say | Do not state that a COA proves safety, efficacy, clinical suitability, or human/animal suitability. |

**Suggested outline**

1. What a COA is in a research procurement context
2. Product and lot identifiers
3. Purity vs identity
4. HPLC purity: what it shows and what it does not
5. MS/LC-MS identity confirmation
6. Batch/lot traceability checklist
7. Red flags: missing lot, no method, no chromatogram, recycled documents, no lab identity
8. How to request documentation from us
9. Research-use-only disclaimer

### 2. HPLC vs LC-MS Explained

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Explain why both purity and identity matter. |
| Audience | Buyers comparing suppliers, SEO visitors, documentation reviewers. |
| Search intent | “HPLC vs LC-MS peptides,” “peptide purity testing,” “mass spectrometry peptide identity.” |
| Why it builds trust | Competitors cite HPLC/MS frequently; explaining it makes the site authoritative and AI-answer-friendly. |
| What not to say | Do not imply lab testing makes products fit for human, animal, diagnostic, or therapeutic use. |

**Suggested outline**

1. Quick answer block: HPLC helps evaluate purity; LC-MS/MS helps confirm molecular identity
2. What HPLC measures
3. What LC-MS/MS measures
4. Why purity and identity are different
5. What a strong documentation packet may include
6. Limitations and why methods matter
7. Link to COA guide and product records

### 3. Research-Use-Only Purchasing Guide

### 4. How Batch Documentation Works

### 6. Storage and Handling Documentation Overview

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Address storage documentation without giving use/protocol instructions. |

| Why it builds trust | Many competitors include storage notes; we can provide cautious documentation framing. |

## AEO/GEO formatting rules

Use these blocks on every trust page:

- 40–70 word “Quick answer” at top.
- Short definition box.
- Checklist table.
- FAQ section with 4–6 questions.
- “What this page does not cover” disclaimer.
- Internal links to product records, quality page, policy, and inquiry.
- Schema: `Article`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`, `Organization`. Avoid medical schema.
