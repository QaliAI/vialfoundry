# 06 — Affiliate Marketing Landscape

*Labels: [FACT]/[OBSERVED]/[INFERENCE]/[REQUIRES COUNSEL]. Nothing here is legal advice; affiliate agreements and disclosure language require attorney review.*

## How peptide affiliate/referral marketing appears to work

- **[OBSERVED]:** Vendors run affiliate programs via platforms like Refersion or UpPromote, issuing each affiliate a **referral link + coupon code**. Commissions observed: 10% (Onyx, Midwest, Spartan), 15% default scaling to 20–25% (Purity), 20% base with AOV >$150 (Apollo), 20% first-order / 10% recurring (Amino Club). Cookie windows ~30 days.
- **[OBSERVED]:** "Best peptide vendor" **review sites** (Outliyr, PeptidePick, Muscle and Brawn, SARMGuide) are themselves affiliates — their rankings double as funnels. Independent rating sites (Finnrick, Best Peptide Brands) position on COA/lab-test credibility.
- **[OBSERVED]:** Coupon aggregators (SimplyCodes) passively redistribute codes.
- **[OBSERVED]:** Reddit/forums (r/Peptides) and biohacking creators drive significant word-of-mouth but are saturated with human-use claims.

## Common strategies observed

1. **Coupon-code seeding:** unique codes give followers 10–15% off; affiliate earns 10–20%.
2. **Review-site rankings:** ranked "top vendor" lists with embedded affiliate links.
3. **SEO affiliate articles:** "[Vendor] review," "[Vendor A] vs [Vendor B]," "best peptide for X."
4. **Forum/Reddit influence:** organic mentions, AMAs, community trust.
5. **Creator sponsorships:** YouTube/podcast/newsletter placements with sponsor codes.

## Risks of influencers in this category

- **[FACT]:** FTC treats influencer marketing as a regulated advertising channel (2025 enforcement focus on health/wellness). Material connections (payment, free product, affiliate links, employment) **must** be clearly and conspicuously disclosed *with the endorsement itself* — not buried in bios or behind "more."
- **[FACT]:** Endorsers cannot make claims the advertiser couldn't substantiate (e.g., that a product treats a health condition). A brand can be liable for an affiliate's false claims.
- **[INFERENCE]:** For a research-use brand, the biggest danger is an affiliate converting our clean catalog into a human-use pitch ("I used this to lose weight/heal my shoulder"). That single post can feed an FDA "totality of marketing" case. See `09`.

## FTC disclosure requirements (high level) — [FACT]

- Disclose any material connection clearly, conspicuously, in the same place as the claim.
- "Paid partnership" tags alone are insufficient if not visible in-content.
- Disclosures on profile/about pages or after "more" are inadequate.
- Truthful + substantiated claims required; no invented health claims.
- *(Source: FTC Endorsement Guides; Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers.)*

## Copy guardrails for affiliates (what they must NOT say)

Prohibited affiliate claims (non-exhaustive):
- Any human-use, dosing, reconstitution, or injection language.
- Weight loss / fat loss / muscle gain / healing / recovery / anti-aging / performance / libido / sleep claims.
- GLP-1 brand comparisons ("like Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro," "same active ingredient").
- Personal-outcome testimonials ("this fixed my…", "I lost X lbs").
- Disease/treatment/diagnosis claims (diabetes, glucose, fertility, cancer, etc.).
- Anything implying the product is for human or animal consumption.

## Affiliate partner criteria (who we accept)

| Accept | Reject |
|---|---|
| Documentation/quality-focused reviewers (Finnrick, Best Peptide Brands style) | Creators who make human-use/outcome claims |
| B2B/research-context content creators | Weight-loss/bodybuilding-claim channels |
| Audiences aligned with research framing | Anyone unwilling to follow disclosure + claim rules |
| Affiliates who agree in writing to copy guardrails | Offshore/anonymous operators we can't vet |

## High-risk influencer red flags

- Existing posts with weight-loss/muscle/healing claims.
- "Before/after" body content; injection imagery.
- GLP-1 brand-name comparisons.
- No FTC disclosures on past sponsored content.
- Unwillingness to sign claim/disclosure terms.

## Recommended "safe-ish" referral structure [INFERENCE — requires counsel]

- **Start with a curated, contractual program** (not open self-serve) so every affiliate signs guardrails.
- Tiered commission (e.g., 10% base, up to 20% for compliant top performers), 30-day cookie, auto-applied codes.
- Mandatory FTC disclosure language + a **prohibited-claims appendix** in the agreement.
- Right to terminate + claw back commissions for guardrail violations.
- Monitoring: periodic review of affiliate content; takedown process.

## Outreach prioritization model

| Priority | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Independent COA/rating sites (Finnrick, Best Peptide Brands) | Credibility, low claim risk, fits positioning |
| 2 | Documentation-focused reviewers willing to use research framing | Reach + acceptable risk |
| 3 | B2B/research newsletters & communities | Aligned audience |
| 4 (caution) | General biohacking creators | Reach but high claim risk; only with strict terms |
| Avoid | Bodybuilding/weight-loss claim channels | Regulatory liability |
