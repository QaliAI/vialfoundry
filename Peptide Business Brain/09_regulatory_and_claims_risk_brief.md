# 09 — Regulatory & Claims Risk Brief

*Management-level brief. **This is not legal advice.** It summarizes public FDA/FTC sources and observed enforcement patterns. Every item marked [REQUIRES COUNSEL] needs an FDA/FTC-experienced attorney. Do not treat any structure described here as "legal" or "compliant."*

---

## The core concept: totality of marketing

**[FACT]** The FDA does not evaluate a product's "research use only" (RUO) label in isolation. It looks at the **totality of evidence** — product pages, blog posts, social media, cart contents, testimonials, metatags — to determine the seller's *intended use*. If the totality implies human consumption, the product is treated as an unapproved (and often misbranded) drug regardless of disclaimers.

**[FACT]** As summarized by counsel commentary on the 2025 letters: the FDA stated the RUO disclaimer "didn't matter" when product pages described appetite suppression, weight loss, and glucose regulation, and when bacteriostatic water for injection sat in the same cart. The RUO disclaimer is treated as a "ruse" when other evidence suggests human consumption. *(Sources: FDA warning letters; HealthLaw Alliance; Holland & Knight; Wilson Sonsini.)*

## FDA enforcement patterns (timeline of public actions) — [FACT]

- **Dec 10, 2024:** Warning letter to **Summit Research Peptides** (695607).
- **Dec 2024 cluster:** Letters to **Prime Peptides, Xcel Peptides, SwissChems, Summit Research** (semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide as unapproved drugs).
- **Feb 26, 2025:** Warning letter to **USApeptide.com** (696885).
- **June 2025:** **Amino Asylum** reported raided by FDA.
- **Sept 9, 2025:** **50+ warning letters** to GLP-1 compounders/manufacturers and RUO peptide sellers (e.g., GLP-1 Solution, 715883); targeted semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, plus BPC-157 and SARMs; cited false/misleading "same active ingredient"/"generic version" claims.
- **March 2026:** **Peptide Sciences** (established, relatively compliance-minded) **voluntarily shut down** amid pressure.

**[INFERENCE]** The trajectory is one-directional: escalating frequency, broadening from GLP-1 to general RUO peptides, and moving from letters to raids/shutdowns.

## Why "research use only" disclaimers have limits — [FACT]

- RUO is meaningful only if the *entire* presentation is genuinely research-oriented.
- Contradictory copy anywhere (site, blog, social, email, affiliate, reviews) can negate it.
- Selling injection supplies (bacteriostatic water, syringes, needles) is strong evidence of human-use intent.

## Higher-risk product categories — [INFERENCE from enforcement]

1. **GLP-1s (semaglutide/tirzepatide/retatrutide)** — highest; epicenter of enforcement. *Recommend exclusion.*
2. **BPC-157 / TB-500** — high, due to typical healing/recovery marketing by others.
3. **GH secretagogues** — high, due to muscle/anti-aging marketing.
4. **Metabolic/mitochondrial** — elevated if tied to weight/energy.
5. Anything sold alongside injection supplies — elevated regardless of compound.

## Copy & claims to AVOID (hard rules across ALL surfaces)

No language implying: human use · animal use · disease treatment/diagnosis (diabetes, glucose, insulin, fertility, cancer, etc.) · weight/fat loss · muscle gain · healing/recovery/injury repair · anti-aging · performance/libido/sleep/wellness outcomes · dosing · reconstitution · injection · protocols/stacks/cycles · GLP-1 brand comparisons ("same as," "generic," "alternative to" Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro) · testimonials implying personal outcomes. No sale of bacteriostatic water, syringes, or needles.

## Why injection supplies increase risk — [FACT]

FDA explicitly cited bacteriostatic water "in the same cart" as evidence of human-use intent. Cross-selling injection supplies converts a catalog into evidence of an unapproved-drug operation. **Hard exclusion.**

## Channel-specific risks

- **Social media [FACT/FTC]:** posts implying outcomes are part of the totality; FTC also regulates endorsement claims.
- **Affiliate/influencer:** a partner's human-use claim can be attributed to the brand. See `06`.
- **Email marketing:** promotional copy is marketing evidence — same guardrails apply.
- **Product reviews/testimonials:** on-site reviews implying human outcomes are risky; moderate them.
- **Payment processors:** independently terminate for claim language (Visa/MC BRAM) — a second kill-switch.

## Recommended website controls [INFERENCE — confirm with counsel]

- Centralized, enforced copy guardrails (lint/checklist) across site, blog, email templates, schema.
- Research-record-only product pages; no outcome language; prominent, honest RUO policy page.
- Controlled inquiry/access workflow + research-use acknowledgement at submission (already in MVP plan).
- Review moderation to strip human-outcome testimonials.
- No injection supplies in catalog; no GLP-1 marketing.
- Don't claim the site is "FDA compliant," "legal," or "payment approved."

## Recommended affiliate controls

Contractual prohibited-claims appendix, FTC disclosure mandate, monitoring, termination + clawback. See `06`.

## Recommended client / legal review points [REQUIRES COUNSEL]

1. Final category inclusion/exclusion (especially any systemic compounds).
2. Product-page copy templates and the RUO policy wording.
3. Whether/how an RUO sales structure can be defensibly operated, and under what entity/terms.
4. Affiliate agreement + prohibited-claims appendix.
5. Email and social copy frameworks.
6. State-level and import/export considerations.
7. Insurance (product liability) and corporate structuring.

> **Bottom line [INFERENCE]:** The brands that survive will be the ones whose *entire* public footprint is genuinely research/documentation-oriented and who stay out of GLP-1 and injection-supply territory. Our positioning is designed for exactly that — but it must be validated by counsel before launch, and discipline must be permanent, not launch-day only.
